# Country attribute data – sources and licences

`countries.json` (country-level facts for the map filters) and `minerals.json` (natural
resources by producer) are generated by `scripts/data/attributes/build_attributes.py`
from the datasets below (all fetched on 2026-11-05; HDI, happiness and plug types refreshed the same day once the build machine could reach Our World in Data and Wikipedia). Every field in `countries.json`
carries its own `source`, `license`, `year` and `note`.

## Attribution line for the app

Short form (e.g. in the legend or "About" panel):

> Country data: CIA World Factbook · World Bank (CC BY 4.0) · Our World in Data (CC BY 4.0;
> Global Carbon Project, Ember, Energy Institute, UNDP, World Happiness Report, NCD-RisC) ·
> Natural Earth · mledoze/countries (ODbL 1.0) · Unicode CLDR · country-coder ·
> IANA tz database · Olympedia · samayo/country-json · countries gem ·
> Wikipedia "Mains electricity by country" (CC BY-SA 4.0) ·
> mineral shares approximated from USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025.

Per field, show `fields[id].source` (and `license`) next to the legend; fields whose
source is `compiled` were assembled by hand from well-established facts (list below).

### ODbL notice (mledoze/countries)

The fields `capital`, `area_km2`, `landlocked`, `borders`, `neighbours_count`, `un_member`,
`independent`, `languages`, `currency`, `calling_code`, `calling_zone` and `tld` are derived from
mledoze/countries (Open Database License 1.0). Keep the attribution, and if the derived
database (`countries.json`) is redistributed, offer it under the ODbL as well.

## Compiled fields and corrections (`"source": "compiled"`)

Compiled by hand from well-established facts (no invented precise numbers):

* `language_family`, `language_branch` – standard genealogical classification of each official
  language (Glottolog/Ethnologue top-level families; creoles, pidgins and sign languages grouped).
* `script` – the script each official language is officially written in (`LANG_SCRIPT` in
  `compiled_languages.py`), for the first language in `languages`; the Writing systems filter. Countries that
  use more than one script are named in the field's note.
* `languages`: Austria and Switzerland use German (`deu`) instead of mledoze's dialect codes
  (`bar`, `gsw`).
* main-language order in `languages` – CLDR speaker shares, with hand adjustments where a lingua
  franca would otherwise come first (e.g. Luxembourgish, Filipino, Swahili in Kenya/Tanzania,
  Kazakh, Papiamento in Aruba, Comorian).
* `organizations` – full members as of November 2026 of 46 organisations: worldwide (UN 193, WTO 165,
  ICC 125, Non-Aligned Movement 120, OIC 57, Commonwealth 56, Francophonie 50, CPLP 9), economic clubs
  (G7, G20, OECD 38, BRICS 10, OPEC 11, APEC 21), military and security (NATO 32, CSTO 6, Five Eyes,
  Quad, AUKUS), Europe (EU 27, eurozone 21, Schengen 29, Council of Europe 46, EFTA, Nordic Council,
  Visegrád Group, Benelux), the Americas (OAS 33, CELAC 33, CARICOM 15, Mercosur 5, Andean Community,
  USMCA), Africa (African Union 55, SADC 16, ECOWAS 12, EAC 8, Alliance of Sahel States 3), the
  Middle East (Arab League 22, GCC), Asia and the Pacific (ASEAN 11, SCO 10, Pacific Islands Forum 18)
  and Eurasia (CIS 9, EAEU 5, Organization of Turkic States 5). Lists come from the organisations'
  own pages where reachable, otherwise from Wikipedia permanent links (September–November 2026),
  cross-checked. Recent changes included: the UAE left OPEC on 1 May 2026; Hungary revoked its ICC
  withdrawal before it took effect (May 2026); Chile left the Non-Aligned Movement (August 2026);
  Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger left ECOWAS (January 2025) and the Francophonie. Suspended members
  count as members (e.g. in the African Union), except Venezuela in Mercosur (suspended since 2016).
  Members that are not countries (the EU in the G7, G20 and WTO, the African Union in the G20, and
  Québec, New Brunswick and Wallonia-Brussels in the Francophonie) are not mapped.
* `monarchy` (43 sovereign monarchies incl. the 15 Commonwealth realms), `federal` (Forum of
  Federations list), `nuclear_weapons` (9 states; Israel undeclared).
* `world_cup_titles` (men's, 1930–2026; Spain's second title in 2026), `womens_world_cup_titles` (1991–2023).
* `religion`: Islamic branch defaults where the factbook gives none (Iran, Iraq, Azerbaijan, Bahrain:
  Shia; Oman: Ibadi; others Sunni), Christian denomination for Canada, Bulgaria, Cuba, Namibia;
  Panama (factbook entry duplicates Honduras), North Korea (none), St Kitts and Nevis (no entry).
* `government_type` corrections where the factbook text is outdated or ambiguous: Switzerland

* `minerals.json` mineral shares (all except oil, natural gas and coal): approximate world shares,

## minerals.json

`{ id: { en, es, emoji, unit, unit_es, year, source, [license], [approximate], [note], producers: [[ISO3, % of world], …] } }`

## Known gaps and caveats

* No reachable open dataset for **plug types / mains voltage** – those fields were skipped rather
  than guessed.
* HDI is the 2019 edition (newest available from a reachable open dataset); happiness is the
  World Happiness Report 2022; heights refer to people born in 1996.
* Olympic medals run through Milano Cortina 2026 (Wikipedia's all-time table: no art competitions,
  no 1906 Intercalated Games, stripped medals as the IOC counts them) and are not attributed to
  successor states of the Soviet Union, East/West Germany, Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, or to Russia
  for ROC/OAR/AIN medals.
* The World Factbook stopped being updated in early 2026; some entries (e.g. government types)
  lag recent events. Survey years differ between countries for literacy, Gini, religions.
* `avg_temperature` (samayo/country-json) has no documented original source or reference period.
* `vat_rate` only covers EU/EEA countries, the UK and Switzerland.


## regional-languages.json (Regional languages filter)

Languages spoken at home in 1,592 first-level regions (Natural Earth admin-1, as drawn by the app) of
137 countries, 680 languages with English and Spanish names, family and approximate L1 speakers.
Each region lists its languages, the one most people speak at home first, then other languages that
are widely spoken or co-official there; a few secondary entries are smaller minorities and say so in
their note (Breton, Low German, Nahuatl, Scots). Regions that only speak the national main language
are omitted. Built by `scripts/data/attributes/regional/build_regional.py` from the hand-compiled
tables next to it (`rl_europe.py`, `rl_asia.py`, `rl_africa.py`, `rl_americas.py`, `rl_langs.py`,
`rl_notes.py`); the sources are listed in `scripts/data/attributes/regional/SOURCES.txt`.

* Census or survey figures where they exist: Catalonia EULP 2023 (IDESCAT), Galicia IGE 2023, ISTAT
  2015, INEGI 2020, Guatemala 2018, Peru 2017, Bolivia 2024, Pakistan 2023, Paraguay 2024, Taiwan
  2020, Canada, the Balkans, the Baltics and more (many via citypopulation.de).
* Estimates from ethnolinguistic sources (Wikipedia and its references) where no census asks about
  language: much of Africa, Iran, Turkey, Myanmar and Tanzania. These are the least certain.
* Codes are ISO 639-3; `nah` (Nahuatl) and `ber` are collective codes. "zho" is shown as
  "Chinese (Mandarin)". Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0 (Wikipedia-derived compilation).
